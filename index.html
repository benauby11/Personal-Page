Catan, rebuilt from scratch

Java · team project · Northwestern CS

A full implementation of the Catan board game, built with a team under strict engineering discipline: test-driven development, boundary value analysis, EasyMock for isolating dependencies, and SpotBugs and CheckStyle enforced on every commit.

I worked on the core game model — classes like BoardHandler, Port, TradeManager, TradeOffer, ResourceDeck, and GameModel — and wrote the integration and unit tests that kept the whole thing honest as the codebase grew.

A guitar you play on a Micro:bit

Embedded C · Micro:bit v2

A Guitar Hero–style instrument running on bare embedded hardware. Guitar chord recordings get converted into C header arrays and played back through PWM — no audio codec, no sample library, just square waves shaped closely enough to sound like real chords.

Most of the real work was in the failure modes: linker errors, duplicate symbols, and the general chaos of pushing audio through a microcontroller that was never designed to make music.

An underwater scene, rendered from nothing

WebGL · GLSL · CS 351, Northwestern

A from-scratch 3D underwater world for a computer graphics course: OBJ model parsing, hand-laid-out vertex buffers, Phong lighting, texture mapping, caustic light patterns on the seafloor, volumetric light rays through the water, and a skybox cubemap holding it all together.

Jellyfish drift, sharks and goldfish swim on their own animation cycles, and a full camera system lets you move through the scene. The codebase went through a full refactor into modular files after a long stretch of chasing down rendering, shader, and buffer bugs.
